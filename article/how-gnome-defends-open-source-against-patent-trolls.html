How GNOME Defends Open Source Against Patent Trolls

The GNOME Foundation plays a critical role in shielding the open-source community from patent assertion entities, commonly known as patent trolls. By choosing to actively litigate rather than settle frivolous infringement claims, the foundation not only protects its own desktop environment and associated applications but also establishes vital legal precedents that safeguard the broader Free and Open Source Software (FOSS) ecosystem.

Patent trolls exploit the high cost of litigation by targeting open-source projects with vague, broad software patents, hoping non-profit organizations will pay a nominal settlement fee to avoid court costs. Because most open-source projects rely on community donations and lack dedicated legal departments, these predatory tactics pose an existential threat to community-driven development.

The GNOME Foundation demonstrated how to effectively counter this threat in 2019, when Rothschild Patent Imaging (RPI) accused the foundation of infringing on a generic wireless image-sharing patent through the Shotwell photo management application. Instead of paying a settlement to make the lawsuit disappear, the GNOME Foundation retained legal counsel, rallied community support, and launched a vigorous defense to invalidate the patent entirely.

This counteroffensive culminated in a historic 2020 settlement. The GNOME Foundation secured not only a complete dismissal of the case with zero financial payout, but also a binding release and patent commitment for any software released under an Open Source Initiative (OSI) approved license. This expanded protection ensured that RPI could not use its patent portfolio against any other open-source developer or project using OSI-approved terms.

Through this proactive stance, the GNOME Foundation shifted the risk dynamics for patent trolls attacking FOSS developers. By setting a precedent of non-compliance and community-funded resistance, the foundation proved that open-source organizations can fight back successfully, deterring predatory lawsuits and reinforcing the legal boundaries that allow free software to thrive.
