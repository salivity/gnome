GNOME Foundation Bylaws and Voting Eligibility

The GNOME Foundation Bylaws serve as the foundational legal instrument establishing the criteria, processes, and rights associated with general membership and voting eligibility. By explicitly defining who qualifies as a voting member, how membership is acquired and renewed, and how voter rolls are maintained, the Bylaws ensure that governance of the GNOME project remains democratic and centered on its active contributor base.

Establishing Contributor-Based Eligibility

The Bylaws mandate that general membership is reserved for individuals who have made a significant, non-trivial contribution to the GNOME project. Unlike organizations where voting shares are purchased or held by corporate sponsors, the Bylaws tie voting franchise directly to personal participation. Eligible contributions typically include:

  • Writing or reviewing code for GNOME software.
  • Documentation, translation, and localization work.
  • Design, usability testing, and artwork creation.
  • Organizing events, marketing, and community advocacy.

By restricting membership to contributors, the Bylaws prevent external entities from acquiring voting control through financial influence.

The Membership and Renewal Process

The Bylaws set forth the mechanism for verifying and maintaining voting eligibility over time. Key procedural safeguards include:

  • The Membership Committee: The Bylaws authorize the creation of a specialized committee responsible for vetting applications, evaluating contributions, and granting membership status.
  • Defined Terms and Expiration: Membership is not permanent. To prevent inactive or departed members from swaying current governance, the Bylaws specify that memberships expire after a set term (typically two years).
  • Renewals: Members must reapply and demonstrate continued involvement to renew their voting status, ensuring that only current stakeholders participate in decision-making.

One Person, One Vote

Under the Bylaws, voting power is distributed on an egalitarian basis. Every approved general member in good standing receives exactly one vote in Foundation elections and referendums. The Bylaws prohibit proxy voting or cumulative voting models, ensuring that all individual voices hold equal weight regardless of employer, tenure, or prominence within the project.

Scope of the Voting Franchise

The Bylaws strictly delineate what general members are entitled to vote on. The primary exercises of voting eligibility include:

  • Electing the Board of Directors: Members select the directors who manage the foundation's strategic, financial, and legal operations.
  • Bylaw Amendments: Significant changes to the Bylaws themselves often require approval or ratification by the voting membership.
  • Referendums: The membership can vote on specific initiatives or advisory questions put forth by the Board or petitioned by the community.

Maintaining the Electoral Roll

Prior to any election, the Bylaws require the official voter roll to be finalized by a specific cutoff date. Individuals whose applications are pending, expired, or approved after the cutoff are ineligible to cast ballots in that specific electoral cycle. This mechanism protects the integrity of election outcomes by preventing last-minute manipulations of the voting pool.
