How GNOME Shell Handles Notification Stacking

GNOME Shell manages simultaneous alerts through a combination of sequential banner displays and consolidated application grouping in the system tray. Rather than cluttering the desktop with multiple overlapping banners when several alerts arrive at once, the desktop environment prioritizes clean visual presentation by queuing transient pop-ups and stacking historical notifications by application within the central calendar menu.

Transient Banner Queuing

When multiple notifications arrive at the exact same moment, GNOME Shell does not display them simultaneously across the screen. Instead, it utilizes an internal queue mechanism:

  • Single-Banner Display: Only one notification banner appears at the top of the screen at any given moment. This prevents visual clutter and keeps user focus intact.
  • FIFO Processing: Standard alerts are generally processed in a First-In, First-Out order. As one banner times out or is dismissed, the next pending alert slides into view.
  • Urgency Overrides: Notifications flagged as critical (such as critical battery warnings or system-level errors) bypass standard queue priorities. Critical banners remain persistent on-screen until explicitly acknowledged or dismissed by the user, while low-priority banners are deferred.

Grouping in the Notification List

Once banners fade or if alerts arrive while the user is actively working, the notifications are stored in the Message Tray, accessible by clicking the clock or pressing Super + V.

  • Application-Based Stacks: Notifications are grouped strictly by the application that generated them. If an application (such as an email client or messaging app) generates five notifications in quick succession, they are collapsed under a single application header.
  • Collapsible Views: In modern GNOME releases, multiple alerts from a single source appear as a layered or expandable card. Users can expand the stack to inspect individual messages or collapse it to save space.
  • Batch Actions: Stacking allows for collective actions. Users can clear an entire stack from a specific application with a single click on that application's close button, or clear all stacked notifications across all apps using the global "Clear" button.

User Interaction and Do Not Disturb

If the "Do Not Disturb" mode is toggled on, the sequential banner queue is bypassed entirely. Incoming simultaneous alerts produce no visual banners or audio cues; instead, they route immediately and silently into their respective application stacks inside the notification menu, awaiting manual review.
