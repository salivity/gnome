How GNOME Contacts Merges Duplicate Entries

GNOME Contacts simplifies address book management by detecting and combining redundant contact records into a unified view. This article explores the core features and underlying architecture—most notably the integration with the Folks library, Evolution Data Server, and attribute-matching heuristics—that empower GNOME Contacts to automatically identify and merge duplicate address entries across multiple local and online accounts.

The libfolks Persona and Individual Model

The primary feature enabling automatic merging in GNOME Contacts is its reliance on libfolks, a contact aggregation library. Instead of treating contact records as flat database entries, libfolks introduces a two-tier data model:

  • Personas: The individual contact records sourced directly from specific backends (such as a local vCard file, Google Contacts, or Nextcloud).
  • Individuals: The composite contacts presented to the user, constructed by automatically grouping related Personas together.

When GNOME Contacts launches or syncs with a service, libfolks analyzes the incoming personas and merges them into a single individual entry if matching rules are satisfied, without overwriting the raw data on the original service.

Normalized Multi-Attribute Matching Heuristics

GNOME Contacts relies on automated heuristic algorithms to determine whether two or more entries represent the same real-world person. The matching engine evaluates several standardized criteria:

  • Normalized Email Addresses: Identical email addresses serve as strong unique identifiers, triggering an immediate merge regardless of slight differences in name formatting.
  • Phone Number Normalization: Phone numbers are parsed and matched according to international dialing formats (E.164 standardization), ignoring discrepancies caused by spaces, dashes, or country codes.
  • Fuzzy Name Matching: The software compares display names, given names, and family names, tolerating minor typographical differences or alternate ordering (e.g., "Doe, John" versus "John Doe").

When these attributes cross a predefined confidence threshold, the application links the entries automatically.

Evolution Data Server (EDS) Multi-Account Integration

The backend power behind GNOME Contacts is the Evolution Data Server (EDS). EDS centralizes data from multiple protocols, including CardDAV, Microsoft Exchange, and local address books. By routing all contact streams through EDS into libfolks, GNOME Contacts does not need manual import/export routines to find duplicates. It cross-references contacts across completely different cloud providers in real time, merging duplicates that exist between separate platforms (such as a Google contact and a Nextcloud contact) into a single cohesive profile.

Non-Destructive Merging Mechanism

A critical characteristic of the merging feature in GNOME Contacts is that it is non-destructive. When the system merges duplicate entries automatically:

  • No source data is permanently deleted or overwritten on the remote servers.
  • Conflicting information (such as different mailing addresses or secondary phone numbers) is combined additively within the unified profile.
  • If an automated merge occurs in error, the underlying architecture retains the original persona boundaries, allowing users to manually unlink the combined records at any time.
