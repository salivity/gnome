How GNOME Shell Handles Full-Screen Notifications

GNOME Shell automatically suppresses intrusive notification banners whenever an application is running in full-screen mode to prevent interruptions during video games, movies, and presentations. Instead of displaying pop-up toasts over active windows, the desktop environment routes non-urgent alerts directly to the notification center while preserving full-screen performance and immersion. This article explains how GNOME Shell detects full-screen applications, processes different notification urgency levels, and handles user controls during full-screen sessions.

Automatic Full-Screen Detection

GNOME’s window and compositing manager, Mutter, continuously monitors window states across both Wayland and X11 sessions. When an application requests full-screen mode—such as a video player, slide deck, or video game—Mutter marks the window surface as covering the entire monitor geometry.

Once a window enters this state, GNOME Shell temporarily disables the standard pop-up banner overlay mechanism for that display. This avoids visual distractions and prevents the compositor from breaking direct scanout (unredirection) in games, which ensures minimal input latency and optimal frame rates.

The Notification Queue and Message Tray

Notifications received while an application is full-screen are not lost or discarded. Instead, GNOME Shell modifies how they are delivered:

  • Banners Are Suppressed: Visual banners (toasts) that normally slide down from the top panel are blocked from rendering over the full-screen window.
  • Direct Archival: Notifications bypass the screen overlay and are sent directly to the Calendar and Notification menu located in the top panel.
  • Audio Alerts: Depending on the application's configuration and whether "Do Not Disturb" is active, notification sounds may still play softly unless the notification source is specifically muted.

Once you exit full-screen mode or switch workspaces, the notifications remain safely stored in the notification list for you to review at your convenience.

Urgency Levels and Exceptions

The FreeDesktop notification specification defines three urgency levels: Low, Normal, and Critical. GNOME Shell treats these tiers differently when full-screen applications are running:

  1. Low and Normal Urgency: Standard notifications—such as new emails, chat messages, or system update reminders—are quietly filed away without interrupting your application.
  2. Critical Urgency: Alerts categorized as critical (such as an imminent battery failure or hardware warnings) can override standard suppression. These alerts are deemed essential to the operation of the system and may break through full-screen barriers to prevent hardware shutdown or data loss.

Manual Overrides and Do Not Disturb

If you want to ensure that zero banners or audio cues disrupt your session, GNOME includes a built-in Do Not Disturb toggle located in the notification tray (accessible via Super + V or clicking the clock). Turning this on suppresses all notification banners across the entire desktop, regardless of whether an app is running in full-screen mode or windowed mode, ensuring completely distraction-free gaming and presentation environments.
