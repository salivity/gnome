How GNOME Shell Manages Do Not Disturb Notifications

This article examines how GNOME Shell handles desktop alerts when the "Do Not Disturb" (DND) feature is enabled. It covers the suppression of visible pop-ups, the silent logging of incoming alerts to the central message tray, the handling of high-priority critical system warnings, and how the desktop maintains productivity without causing information loss.

Banner Suppression and Sound Muting

When "Do Not Disturb" is toggled on in GNOME Shell, the most immediate change is the suppression of standard notification banners (often called "toast" notifications). Under normal conditions, incoming messages from applications such as email clients, web browsers, or chat platforms appear briefly at the top center of the screen. In DND mode, the Shell intercepts these events and blocks the visual pop-up animation entirely. Additionally, audio alerts linked to incoming notifications are muted to prevent auditory distractions.

Silent Queuing in the Notification List

Suppression does not mean notifications are discarded. GNOME Shell acts as the display server for the org.freedesktop.Notifications D-Bus specification. When an application sends a notification signal, GNOME Shell acknowledges the payload and immediately writes it to the notification store.

Instead of showing on the desktop:

  • The notification is silently deposited into the Notification Drawer, which is embedded inside the Date & Time / Calendar menu on the top bar.
  • The system indicator dot next to the clock is suppressed or modified, ensuring no visual cues interrupt full-screen tasks or meetings.
  • Users can access these queued items at any time by clicking the clock or pressing the Super + V keyboard shortcut.

Critical and Urgent Exceptions

GNOME Shell differentiates notifications based on their urgency level: low, normal, and critical.

While low and normal priority notifications (like chat pings or calendar reminders) are strictly silenced, critical notifications may bypass standard suppression rules depending on system state. Critical alerts typically involve urgent hardware or power events, such as:

  • Imminent battery depletion requiring an immediate shutdown or suspend.
  • Severe system errors, disk failure alerts, or thermal throttling warnings.

These critical alerts are designed to prevent hardware damage or unexpected data loss, ensuring that life-safety and hardware-safety operations remain visible.

Restoration and Deactivation Behavior

When the user turns off "Do Not Disturb," GNOME Shell does not barrage the screen by replaying missed banners all at once. Flooding the display with accumulated banners would defeat the purpose of a clean workflow. Instead, any notifications that arrived while DND was active simply remain organized inside the Notification Drawer until the user manually dismisses them, interacts with them, or clears the list using the "Clear All" button.
